3. What GutDiary is, and what it isn't

GutDiary is a food and symptom diary. The optional analysis uses AI to look for patterns in what you've logged and suggest foods to leave out for a while to see if you feel better.

It isn't a medical device and it doesn't diagnose anything. An analysis is a set of possibilities worked out from your own notes, and it can be wrong. It isn't a substitute for advice from a doctor, dietitian or other health professional, and you shouldn't delay getting that advice because of anything the app says.

  • Allergies: GutDiary is for suspected intolerances, not allergies. If you have or suspect a food allergy, don't use the app to test it. Never eat a food to see whether it causes a reaction.
  • Emergencies: if you have swelling of the lips, tongue or throat, trouble breathing, or feel faint after eating, call 999 (or your local emergency number) straight away.
  • See a doctor if you have blood in your stool, unexplained weight loss, persistent vomiting, difficulty swallowing, severe or worsening pain, or symptoms that wake you at night. See one too if an analysis suggests wheat or gluten might be involved, because coeliac disease has to be tested for before you cut gluten out.
  • Before big changes: talk to a doctor or registered dietitian before cutting out foods for a long time, especially if you're pregnant, have a medical condition or take medication.

The app only ever suggests leaving foods out. It will never tell you to eat something to test it. Whatever you decide to eat is your choice.

4. Your diary

An analysis is only as good as what you log. Photo descriptions are suggestions written by AI, so check each one and add anything the photo can't show, like butter, oil, sauces or dressings. Product ingredients can change, so check the label if it matters to you.
